# Pentagon sign statistics for twisted group algebras of F_2^n

**Status: hand proof plus computational checks. Not reviewed by a mathematician. No claim of novelty or priority** (the literature search was partial; see `THEOREM.md`, section 7). Developed with several AI systems (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok) directed by a human author. Agreement between AI systems is not treated as evidence; please check the proof yourself, and please tell us if it is wrong or already known.

## What is claimed

Let `sigma` be a normalized sign cochain on `F_2^n` with `e_x e_y = sigma(x,y) e_{x+y}`, and `alpha = delta sigma` the associator sign: `(e_x e_y) e_z = alpha(x,y,z) e_x (e_y e_z)`. For four elements `a,b,c,d` the five reassociation signs around the pentagon of bracketings are

    X1 = alpha(a,b,c)   X2 = alpha(a,b+c,d)   X3 = alpha(b,c,d)   X4 = alpha(a,b,c+d)   X5 = alpha(a+b,c,d)

and `X1 X2 X3 X4 X5 = 1` for every `sigma` (so 0, 2 or 4 of them are -1).

**Theorem (hand proof, `THEOREM.md`).** If `alpha(x,x,z) = alpha(x,z,z) = +1` for all `x,z` (left and right alternativity of the basis-unit associator signs), then for every `n` the joint law of `(X1,...,X5)` over all quadruples is invariant under all 120 permutations of the five coordinates. In particular all ten two-flip patterns occur equally often and all five four-flip patterns occur equally often.

It is an exchangeability statement: it does not give the counts, and necessity of the hypotheses is not claimed.

Examples (all quadruples counted):

| System | n | no flip | each 2-flip pattern | each 4-flip pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| standard Cayley-Dickson (sedenions) | 4 | 15,136 | 2,688 | 4,704 |
| alternate product (a,b)(c,d)=(ac - b*d, da* + bc) | 4 | 26,656 | 3,456 | 864 |
| standard Cayley-Dickson | 5 | 166,576 | 48,720 | 78,960 |

Random normalized cochains without the hypotheses satisfy the parity statement but are not uniform.

## Proof in three lines

1. Specialize the pentagon identity three separate times (`d=c`; `b=a`; `c=b`, each applied to the original identity, not one after another) and use the hypotheses: `alpha(x,y,z) = alpha(x,y+z,z) = alpha(x,x+y,z) = alpha(x+y,y,y+z)` for all triples.
2. These substitutions generate the group of order 24 of invertible 3x3 matrices over F_2 preserving `x+z`; so `alpha` is invariant under it.
3. In the coefficient space F_2^4 of `(a,b,c,d)` the five vectors `q_i` (first plus third coefficient vector of the i-th triple: `a+c, a+d, b+d, a+c+d, a+b+d`) sum to 0 and any four are independent, so every permutation of them is induced by some `psi` in GL_4(F_2) mapping the i-th triple's span `H_i` to `H_pi(i)`; this carries triple i to triple pi(i) up to one of the 24 matrices. Hence `X_i(phi psi) = X_pi(i)(phi)`, and `phi -> phi psi` permutes all quadruples.

The three places to check most carefully: the substitutions (step 1), the order-24 group computation (step 2, a finite check), and the equivariance lemma (step 3). Full details are in `THEOREM.md`.

## What is not claimed

- That the hypotheses hold for every Cayley-Dickson dimension: checked for n = 3,4,5 only.
- That they are necessary as a pair. Neither alone suffices (explicit counterexamples in `scripts/counterexamples.py`), and with one condition the group of guaranteed symmetries has order 2, not 24 (exact computation, `scripts/exact_invariance_groups.py`; `THEOREM.md` section 6a); whether some other weaker condition suffices is open.
- That the alternate product is a Cayley-Dickson doubling (it has zero divisors at dimension 8 in our implementation; whether its author withdrew the paper has not been confirmed).
- Novelty or priority.
