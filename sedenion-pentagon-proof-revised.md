## 1. Conventions and notation
- Units e_x, x in F_2^4 (4-bit integers, XOR as addition, e_0 = 1). Cayley-Dickson doubling, with conjugation (p,q)‾ = (p̄, -q):
  (p,q)(r,s) = (pr - s̄ q, sp + q r̄).
  The index of a product is the XOR of the indices: e_x e_y = sigma(x,y) e_{x+y}, sigma = +-1.
- h(x) = top bit of the index x; t = 8 = 1000; the **low half** is {0..7}; pi drops the top bit (pi(x) in F_2^3).
- alpha(x,y,z) in {+1,-1} is defined by (e_x e_y) e_z = alpha e_x (e_y e_z) for index triples (repeats and 0 allowed). Because each unit occurs once on each side, alpha is unchanged if any unit is replaced by its negative: alpha depends only on the indices.
- For an index 4-tuple (a,b,c,d) let phi: F_2^4 -> F_2^4 be the linear map sending the coordinate vectors to a,b,c,d. Its rank is the **rank of the tuple**. Row vector eta = h∘phi, so eta = (h(a),h(b),h(c),h(d)); the top bits of the four operands.
- Notation: "a+b" in an index means XOR.

## 2. Statement
Of the 16^4 = 65,536 ordered index 4-tuples, 15,136 have all five pentagon edge signs +1; each of the ten patterns with exactly two negative edges occurs 2,688 times; each of the five patterns with exactly four negative edges occurs 4,704 times; no other pattern occurs. So 50,400 tuples are sensitive (some bracketings differ in sign). Each index tuple has 16 signed versions with the same pattern, since alpha ignores signs.

## 3. Standard facts used
- **F1 (Cited: Bales, "Periodicity of the Cayley-Dickson twists", Thm 1; Culbert 2007, Thm 5.4).** e_x^2 = -1 for x != 0; e_x e_y = -e_y e_x for distinct nonzero x,y; the conjugate of e_x is -e_x for x != 0. Consequently imaginary units are the units with nonzero index.
- **F0 (Cited: C. Culbert, "Cayley-Dickson algebras and loops", J. Gen. Lie Theory Appl. 1 (2007), Section 5: the basis elements and their negatives form a loop; Prop. 5.3 says each Cayley-Dickson loop satisfies the alternative properties a(ab) = a^2 b and ba^2 = (ba)a; Prop. 5.2 the inversive properties; Thm 5.4: any pair of elements generates a subgroup of the quaternion group. Checked here on all 256 pairs of sedenion units, in the engine convention and the second convention.)** For units, e_x(e_x e_y) = e_x^2 e_y and (e_y e_x)e_x = e_y e_x^2; e_x^2 = -1 for x != 0 (F1). **Important distinction:** this is alternativity of the loop of units (an identity for basis elements and their negatives only). The sedenion algebra itself is not alternative, and nothing here assumes it is. Culbert's convention for the doubling may differ from ours in detail; the 256-pair check is what establishes F0 for the stated convention. (Culbert's Thm 5.4 gives F3 with one more line: if the three indices span a space of dimension 2, two of the units, say x and y, are independent and the third is +-1, +-x, +-y or +-xy, so all three lie in the group generated by x and y, a subgroup of the quaternion group, which is associative; if the span has dimension at most 1 the units are +-1 and +-x, again a group. The explicit computation below is kept so that F3 rests on F0 alone.)
- **F2 (Proof; self-contained given that the octonions are an alternative algebra, a standard fact).** Let a,b,c be octonion units with independent indices u,v,w. Then a(bc) = -(ab)c. *Proof.* Polarising left and right alternativity gives, for all octonions, a(bc) + b(ac) = (ab+ba)c and (ab)c + (ac)b = a(bc+cb). By F1, ab+ba = 0 and bc+cb = 0 (distinct nonzero indices). The units b and ac have different nonzero indices (v versus u+w, different by independence), so b(ac) = -(ac)b by F1. Then
  a(bc) = -b(ac) (first identity, since ab+ba=0) = (ac)b (anticommutation) = -(ab)c (second identity, since bc+cb=0). ∎
  This is the argument of Lemma S' in `sharp-associator-short-proof.md` specialised to units, with the orthogonality hypothesis replaced by independence of the indices. In particular alpha = -1 for independent octonion units.
- **F3 (Proof).** If the three indices span a subspace of dimension at most 2, alpha = +1. *Proof.* Let W be the span, dim at most 2; the units with indices in W are closed under multiplication, so they span a subalgebra Q. If dim W <= 1, Q is spanned by 1 and at most one imaginary unit i with i^2 = -1, which is commutative and associative (C or R). If dim W = 2, take i = e_{w1}, j = e_{w2} and k := ij = +-e_{w1+w2}; here i, j, k are three units with distinct nonzero indices w1, w2, w1+w2, and the sign of k is fixed once and for all as the sign of the product ij, so that "k" is the signed unit ij throughout the table below. Because their indices are distinct and nonzero, F1 applies to every pair among them. By F1, i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = -1 and distinct units among i, j, k anticommute. By F0: i(ij) = i^2 j = -j, (ij)j = i j^2 = -i; with anticommutation: ik = -j, ki = -(ik) = j, jk = j(ij) = -(ij)j = i, kj = -i, ji = -k. So all sixteen products of 1, i, j, k equal the products in the quaternions, and since multiplication on Q is bilinear, Q is isomorphic to H, which is associative. Hence (xy)z = x(yz) for all triples of units in Q, i.e. alpha = +1. ∎
  (This replaces an earlier argument that deduced the quaternion table from anticommutativity and squares alone; that was incomplete, as CG's review pointed out. F0 supplies the missing products.)

## 4. The triple formula
**Lemma 1.** Let x,y,z be indices.
(i) If they span a space of dimension at most 2, alpha = +1.
(ii) If they are independent and t lies in V = span(x,y,z), alpha = -1.
(iii) If they are independent and t is not in V, alpha = -1 exactly when h(x) = h(z).

*(i)* is F3.

*(ii) Proof.* (Argument due to CG; details filled in.) Since t is in V, V has the form W x {0,1}: V = {(0,w),(1,w) : w in W} where W = pi(V) has dimension 2. Let Q be the span of the units e_w, w in W (all of top bit 0). By F1 and the proof of F3, Q is a quaternion algebra: choose an isomorphism psi: H -> Q with psi(i) = e_{w1}, psi(j) = e_{w2}, psi(k) = psi(i)psi(j) = +-e_{w1+w2}. psi preserves the real part (the coefficient of 1) and so conjugation (x̄ = 2 Re x - x). Let D = {(p,q) : p,q in Q}. The doubling formulas use only products and conjugation inside Q, so D is closed under product, and psi~(p,q) = (psi^{-1}p, psi^{-1}q) is an isomorphism from D onto the Cayley-Dickson double of H with the same formula, which is the octonions O. The standard units of O go to +-e_x with x in V, and the induced map on index sets F_2^3 -> V is a bijection compatible with XOR (the index of a product is the XOR), so it is a group isomorphism and preserves independence. By F2 every independent triple of standard units of O has alpha = -1; alpha is invariant under replacing units by their negatives and under the isomorphism. Hence alpha(x,y,z) = -1. ∎

*(iii) Proof.* (Mine.) Since V does not contain t, pi is injective on V, so the low parts u,v,w of x,y,z are independent in F_2^3. Let a = e_u, b = e_v, c = e_w (octonion units) and N = (ab)c. Facts, from F1 and F2, valid because u,v,w are independent: the indices u+v, v+w are nonzero and differ from the third index, so ab, bc, ba, cb are imaginary units, each anticommutes with the unit of different index, and each has conjugate equal to minus itself. This gives
(I1) ba = -ab, cb = -bc;
(I2) a(bc) = -N (F2);
(I3) (bc)a = -a(bc) = N;
(I4) c(ab) = -(ab)c = -N;
(I5) (cb)a = -(bc)a = -N;
(I6) (ba)c = -(ab)c = -N;
(I7) c(ba) = -c(ab) = N;
(I8) a(cb) = -a(bc) = N.
Write a unit with top bit 0 as (e,0) and top bit 1 as (0,e). The four doubling rules (from (p,q)(r,s) = (pr - s̄q, sp + q r̄)) are
LL: (P,0)(R,0) = (PR,0); LH: (P,0)(0,S) = (0,SP); HL: (0,Q)(R,0) = (0,Q R̄); HH: (0,Q)(0,S) = (-S̄Q,0).
Derived products used: xy for the four top-bit pairs on (a,b): LL (ab,0); LH (0,ba); HL (0,-ab); HH (ba,0). yz on (b,c): LL (bc,0); LH (0,cb); HL (0,-bc); HH (cb,0).

|---|---|---|---|
| 000 | (ab,0)(c,0) = (N,0) | (a,0)(bc,0) = (-N,0) by I2 | -1 |
| 001 | (ab,0)(0,c) = (0,c(ab)) = (0,-N) by I4 | (a,0)(0,cb) = (0,(cb)a) = (0,-N) by I5 | +1 |

alpha = -1 exactly in the rows 000, 010, 101, 111, i.e. exactly when h(x) = h(z). ∎

**Proposition 1' (the second common convention is the opposite algebra; Proof).** Let ∘ be the doubling product (p,q)∘(r,s) = (pr - s q̄, p̄ s + r q) (the second common convention). Then x∘y = y·x for all x,y, where · is our product (p,q)(r,s) = (pr - s̄ q, sp + q r̄), at every level of the construction.
*Proof.* By induction on the level, the base case being the reals (commutative). Assume ∘ at level m is the opposite of · at level m, i.e. a∘b = b·a. At level 2m, with · the product of the doubled algebra: (r,s)·(p,q) = (rp - q̄ s, qr + s p̄). Rewriting each lower-level product with the induction hypothesis (rp = p∘r, q̄ s = s∘q̄, qr = r∘q, s p̄ = p̄∘s) gives (p∘r - s∘q̄, p̄∘s + r∘q) = (p,q)∘(r,s) by definition. ∎
*Consequence.* For the opposite algebra alpha_∘(x,y,z) = alpha(z,y,x): indeed (x∘y)∘z = z(yx) and x∘(y∘z) = (zy)x, and (zy)x = alpha(z,y,x) z(yx). The condition in Lemma 1 is symmetric under exchanging x and z (the span V is unchanged and h(x) = h(z) is symmetric), and cases (i),(ii) do not depend on the order, so alpha_∘ = alpha as functions on index triples, and therefore every edge sign, every flip pattern and the whole census of section 8 are identical in the second convention. This upgrades the numerical agreement in that convention (section 9) to a proof. It says nothing about the other Bales products. (Checked on the sedenion units: sigma_∘(x,y) = sigma(y,x) for all 256 pairs; and a sign twist e_x -> eps_x e_x even gives an isomorphism between the two algebras, which was found by search and is not needed.)

## 5. Pentagon bookkeeping (Proof)
The five bracketings of a 4-tuple, in cyclic order, are ((ab)c)d, (a(bc))d, a((bc)d), a(b(cd)), (ab)(cd), with consecutive ones (and the last and first) related by one reassociation. The five edges are, in order, with the edge's triple (x,y,z):
T1 = (a,b,c), T2 = (a,b+c,d), T3 = (b,c,d), T4 = (a,b,c+d), T5 = (a+b,c,d).
All five bracketings land on the unit with index a+b+c+d and differ by signs, so going once round the cycle gives: the product of the five alpha(T_i) equals +1. So the number of negative edges is even: a **flip pattern** F (the set of negative edges) has |F| in {0,2,4}, giving 1 + 10 + 5 = 16 possible patterns.

## 6. The five-point frame (Proof; finite verification written out)
Work in coordinate space F_2^4 with coordinates (a,b,c,d) (so a = 1000 etc. below). For edge i let H_i be the span of the coefficient vectors of its triple:
H_1 = <a,b,c> = {d = 0}; H_2 = <a,b+c,d> = {b = c}; H_3 = <b,c,d> = {a = 0}; H_4 = <a,b,c+d> = {c = d}; H_5 = <a+b,c,d> = {a = b}.
Let q_i = (first) + (last) of the triple i: q_1 = a+c = 1010, q_2 = a+d = 1001, q_3 = b+d = 0101, q_4 = a+c+d = 1011, q_5 = a+b+d = 1101 (as integers with A=1, B=2, C=4, D=8: 5, 9, 10, 13, 11).

**Lemma 2.** (a) q_1+q_2+q_3+q_4+q_5 = 0, and any four of the q_i are linearly independent. (b) Every nonzero vector of F_2^4 is exactly one q_i or exactly one sum q_i+q_j (i<j): 5 + 10 = 15. (c) H_i = {0, q_i} ∪ {q_j+q_l : j,l different from i, j<l}. (d) Hence q_i lies in exactly one of the spans (namely H_i) and q_i+q_j lies in exactly the three H_l with l not in {i,j}.
*Proof.* (a) q_1+...+q_5: add the coordinates column by column: a: 1+1+0+1+1 = 0; b: 0+0+1+0+1 = 0; c: 1+0+0+1+0 = 0; d: 0+1+1+1+1 = 0. For independence: q_1+q_4 = 0001, then q_2+0001 = 1000, q_1+1000 = 0010 and q_3+0001 = 0100, so q_1,...,q_4 span all of F_2^4 and are independent. Since q_5 is the sum of the other four, any four of the five span the same space as all five, namely F_2^4, so any four are independent (a spanning set of four vectors in a four-dimensional space). (b) Five vectors with a single relation: the 5 + 10 = 15 elements are distinct and nonzero (equalities such as q_i+q_j = q_m+q_n or q_i+q_j = q_m would give a relation among at most four of them), hence all 15 nonzero vectors. (c),(d) are a finite check; the table below lists, for each nonzero vector, which H_l contain it (by the coordinate conditions above):

| vector | in H_l for l = | vector | in H_l for l = |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0001 | 2,3,5 | 1000 | 1,2,4 |
| 0010 | 1,3,5 | 1001 (q_2) | 2 |
| 0011 | 3,4,5 | 1010 (q_1) | 1 |
| 0100 | 1,3,4 | 1011 (q_4) | 4 |
| 0101 (q_3) | 3 | 1100 | 1,4,5 |
| 0110 | 1,2,3 | 1101 (q_5) | 5 |

Each q_i is in exactly the one span H_i; each q_i+q_j is in the three spans with l not in {i,j} (for example 0001 = q_1+q_4 is in H_2, H_3, H_5). ∎

For an operand top-bit row eta = (h(a),h(b),h(c),h(d)) put u_i = eta·q_i = h(first_i) + h(last_i) (mod 2). Then u_1+...+u_5 = 0, and since q_1..q_4 are independent, eta is determined by u and u by eta: even-parity u in F_2^5 correspond one-to-one to eta in F_2^4, with eta = 0 exactly when u = 0.

## 7. From a tuple to edge flips (Proof)
**Lemma 3.** Let phi be the tuple's linear map. For edge i:
(a) if rank phi = 3 with kernel <kappa> (kappa != 0), the edge triple is dependent iff kappa is in H_i;
(b) if rank phi = 4, the edge triple is independent, and t lies in its span iff k = phi^{-1}(t) lies in H_i.
*Proof.* The triple is phi applied to the three coefficient vectors spanning H_i. A nontrivial dependency is a nonzero element of H_i killed by phi; so (a) holds because ker phi = {0,kappa}. For (b), phi is injective, and t is in the span phi(H_i) iff phi^{-1}(t) is in H_i. ∎

Consequently, by Lemma 1, the flip set F of a tuple is:
- rank <= 2: F empty (Lemma 1(i), since every edge triple lies in the span of the tuple).
- rank 4: F = {i : k in H_i, or (k not in H_i and u_i = 0)}.
- rank 3, with span V: if t in V or V is the low half: F = {i : kappa not in H_i}; otherwise (V split: t not in V and V not the low half): F = {i : kappa not in H_i and u_i = 0}.
(For V the low half eta = 0, so u_i = 0 and Lemma 1(iii) gives -1 for every independent triple; for t in V Lemma 1(ii) applies. For V split, Lemma 1(iii).)

**Parametrizing tuples (Proof).**
- *Rank 4.* Write phi(e_i) = (eta_i, u_i) (top bit, low part). The map u = pi∘phi: F_2^4 -> F_2^3 is linear and surjective, and its kernel contains k (since pi(t) = 0); because phi is bijective, the kernel is exactly <k>. And eta(k) = h(t) = 1. Conversely, any surjection u with kernel <k> and any eta with eta(k) = 1 give an injective phi (phi(x) = 0 forces u(x) = 0, so x in {0,k}, and phi(k) = (1,0) != 0). So the rank-4 tuples with given k correspond to pairs (u, eta): 168 surjections F_2^4/<k> -> F_2^3 (|GL(3,2)| = 168) times the 8 vectors eta with eta(k) = 1. Total 15 x 168 x 8 = 20,160 = |GL(4,2)|.
- *Rank 3.* Fix a 3-dimensional span V (15 choices). Tuples with image V and kernel <kappa> are the linear isomorphisms F_2^4/<kappa> -> V: 168 of them (a torsor for GL(V)). Total 15 x 168 = 2,520 per V; 15 x 2,520 = 37,800.
- *Rank <= 2:* 65,536 - 20,160 - 37,800 = 7,576 tuples (1 + 225 + 7,350 by rank 0, 1, 2).

## 8. The counts (Proof; arithmetic explicit)
### 8.1 Rank 4
Here eta(k) = 1.
- k = q_i: u_i = eta·q_i = 1. Edge i is forced to flip; the other four edges flip iff their u_j = 0. The other four bits u_j (j != i) have odd sum, so there are 8 assignments (u_j determines eta). The weight of (u_j)_{j != i} is 1 or 3. Weight 3 (four assignments): exactly one j != i has u_j = 0, so the flips are {i,j}, a two-flip pattern, each j != i once. Weight 1 (four assignments): exactly one m != i has u_m = 1, so the flips are all edges except m, a four-flip pattern, each m != i once.
- k = q_i+q_j: u_i+u_j = 1; edges l not in {i,j} are forced; exactly one of i, j flips; 8 assignments (4 for each of the two choices of which of u_i,u_j is 1); the pattern is always a four-flip pattern missing i or missing j.
- Two-flip pattern {i,j}: from k = q_i (once) and k = q_j (once): 2 pairs (k, eta); 2 x 168 = **336** tuples.
- Four-flip pattern missing edge m: from k = q_i, i != m: once each (4); from k = q_m+q_j, j != m: 4 assignments each (16); 20 pairs; 20 x 168 = **3,360** tuples.
- Check: 10 x 336 + 5 x 3,360 = 3,360 + 16,800 = 20,160.

### 8.2 Rank 3, octonion-like spans (8: the low half and the seven containing t)
F = {i : kappa not in H_i}, independent of eta. By Lemma 2(d): kappa = q_i gives F = all edges but i (four flips); kappa = q_i+q_j gives F = {i,j} (two flips). This is a bijection between the 15 values of kappa and the 15 non-empty flip patterns, and each pair (V,kappa) has 168 tuples. So every one of the 15 patterns occurs 8 x 168 = **1,344** times at rank 3 in octonion-like spans. (This replaces the octonion-minor argument of the earlier draft by a self-contained one; it agrees with it.)

### 8.3 Rank 3, split spans (7: the graphs of the seven nonzero linear functionals h on the low F_2^3)
Fix such V. The functional eta = h∘phi is nonzero (V is not the low half) and vanishes on kappa. For fixed kappa the map {tuples with image V, kernel <kappa>} -> {nonzero functionals on F_2^4 vanishing on kappa} (7 of them), tuple -> eta, is GL(V)-equivariant and surjective: the 168 tuples form a GL(V)-torsor, and GL(V) acts transitively on the 7 nonzero functionals of V with stabiliser of order 168/7 = 24 (**transitive, not simply transitive**). So each pair (kappa, eta) is realised by exactly **24** tuples; 15 x 7 = 105 pairs and 105 x 24 = 2,520.
- kappa = q_i: u_i = eta(kappa) = 0; the other four u_j have even sum; the all-zero choice is excluded (it means eta = 0, V = low half); 7 choices. Edge i is dependent and does not flip; edge j flips iff u_j = 0. Weight 4 (one choice): no flips. Weight 2 (six choices): the two zeros give the six two-flip patterns {j,l} with j,l != i, once each. (Weight 0 would give four flips and is excluded.)
- kappa = q_i+q_j: u_i = u_j; the other three edges are dependent; since u sums to 0 and u_i + u_j = 0, the other three u_l have even sum. If u_i = u_j = 1 (4 choices of the u_l) no edge flips; if u_i = u_j = 0 (the u_l even weight, all-zero excluded because eta != 0: 3 choices) the flip set is {i,j}. Total 4 + 3 = 7.
- No flips: 5 x 1 + 10 x 4 = 45 pairs, 45 x 24 = **1,080** tuples per split V. Two-flip pattern {i,j}: 3 (kappa = q_i+q_j) + 3 (kappa = q_l, l not in {i,j}) = 6 pairs, 6 x 24 = **144** tuples per split V. No four-flip pattern occurs. Check: 1,080 + 10 x 144 = 2,520.

### 8.4 Totals
| pattern type | rank 3 (8 octonion-like + 7 split) | rank 4 | all ranks |
|---|---|---|---|
| each of 10 two-flip | 8 x 168 + 7 x 144 = 2,352 | 336 | **2,688** |
| each of 5 four-flip | 8 x 168 = 1,344 | 3,360 | **4,704** |
